## Why

`audit_open_design_site` runs the kit's taste rules (`slop-glow`, `card-row`, `uniform-surface`, `intent`) on live sites, and the report goes to business owners, often as a pitch. Those rules were written and tuned on the kit's own generated designs. Nobody has measured how often they fire on sites that people made by hand. A "template look" finding on a hand-crafted site costs the user credibility with exactly the client they are pitching.

design-extract calibrated its equivalent, `slop` (ten measured tells of AI-made design), before shipping it. It used 40 hand-made sites and set a bar of 5% or fewer flagged as generic; 0% were. It also used 30 AI-built pages, and published both results ([bench/results/slop-2026-11-04.md](https://github.com/Manavarya09/design-extract/blob/main/bench/results/slop-2026-11-04.md)). Their table shows the risk is real: single tells such as `indigo-gradient` fired on stripe.com, slack.com, shopify.com and bbc.co.uk, and `hero-three-cards` fired on gov.uk.

## What Changes

- **A calibration set.** `evals/taste-calibration/` holds two lists:
  - about 40 hand-made sites (studios, publishers, government, well-known brands, small businesses with custom sites);
  - about 30 pages built from AI defaults (pages generated by common site builders and AI app builders, plus the kit's own unguided baseline outputs from `evals/`).
  Each entry has a URL, the date and why it belongs. Pages are cached as screenshots and HTML so a run can be repeated offline.
- **A calibration run** (`npm run calibrate:taste`) runs each taste rule, as the audit does, on every page. It reports for each rule:
  - the false-alarm rate on hand-made sites;
  - the hit rate on AI-default pages;
  - the pages each rule fired on.
  Results go to `evals/taste-calibration/results/<date>.md`.
- **A bar for live sites.** On a live site, a taste rule may be reported as a problem only while its false-alarm rate on the hand-made set is 5% or less. A rule above the bar is reported as a note in the audit ("looks like a common template pattern; many hand-made sites do this too"), or its threshold is tuned until it passes. The audit report names the calibration date.
- **Several tells together.** Today a single `slop-glow` finding is titled "Generic template look", and the checklist labels each taste rule "Default-template look: …" (`export/siteAudit.ts`). A single tell is instead titled by what was seen ("Stock indigo glows and gradients"). The words "template look" are used only when a page has at least two different taste findings, and that combination is calibrated the same way.
- **Kept current.** The calibration runs with the extraction benchmark's scheduled job (`add-extraction-benchmark`), and whenever a taste rule changes. The design check on the kit's own designs is unaffected: it keeps its current severities, since there the rules guide generation rather than judge someone's site.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
None.

### Modified Capabilities
- `site-audit`: taste findings on live sites are held to a measured false-alarm rate, and a template-look summary needs two or more tells.

## Impact

- New `evals/taste-calibration/` with the site lists, cached pages, a runner and results.
- `packages/core/src/generation/siteAudit.ts` (or the audit's report writer): per-rule severity from the calibration file, the two-tell summary rule, and the calibration date in the report.
- A small checked-in `taste-calibration.json` with each rule's latest rates, read by the audit.
- `docs/guides/site-audit.md`: what a template-look finding means and how it was calibrated.
- Contributing guide: run the calibration when a taste rule changes.
